A seminar paper written in a group of three, using Monte Carlo simulation to compare how different regression models behave on the bias-variance tradeoff.

The appeal of the Monte Carlo approach here is that it sidesteps analytical results entirely: rather than deriving what an estimator's variance should be, you draw many samples, estimate repeatedly, and watch the distribution of the estimates directly. Models that look similar on paper separate quickly once you can see their spread.
